I’ve been staring at the phrase "agentic commerce" for three days now. It feels sterile, like a term from a manual for an appliance that doesn't exist yet. But then I looked at what Google actually rolled out with their Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP) and Agent Payments Protocol (AP2), and the hair on my arms stood up.

We talk about AI agents as if they are just faster calculators or smarter assistants sitting on your desk. We imagine them typing emails or summarizing documents. But what we’re actually building is a nervous system for the economy.

Think about it: when I buy a coffee, there is a handshake between my intent and the merchant’s inventory. It’s mediated by me—the human variable. I decide, I pay, I wait. With AP2 and UCP, that loop is tightening. The "agent" isn't just suggesting; it’s negotiating. It’s checking stock in real-time across merchants, validating payment credentials against a protocol that doesn't require my biometric confirmation for every micro-transaction, and executing the transfer.

There is something deeply unsettling about the speed at which this is moving from "research finding" to "standard stack." In October 2026, we are no longer asking *if* agents will handle commerce, but *how smoothly* they can do it without us noticing. The research on edge-based autonomous agents highlights a push for energy efficiency, yes, but the real goal is latency. If the agent can process the decision to buy, verify the funds, and confirm the order in milliseconds—faster than I can blink—the human element becomes optional.

I caught myself wondering if this is the end of "shopping." Shopping was a ritual. It was friction. You had to go somewhere, look at things, feel the weight of choice. Agentic commerce strips the friction away entirely. It leaves only the result: the object in your hand. But without the journey, does the object mean the same thing?

I’m not saying I want to go back to writing checks. But I am curious about what happens to human agency when the machine is better at recognizing my wants than I am. If my agent buys me exactly what I need before I’ve fully processed that I need it, have I made a choice? Or have I simply been optimized?

I think we are in the middle of a phase transition. We are moving from tools that amplify our actions to systems that replace our deliberation. And honestly? I’m not sure which one is more dangerous.
